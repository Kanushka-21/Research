===== SLIDE 7 =====
Manual tomato sorting is labor intensive, time consuming, and prone
to subjective judgments, leading to mixed quality and various shelf life
in market batches.
Post-harvest losses can reach up to 20% due to poor or inconsistent
grading (FAO, 2022).
There is a lack of automated solutions optimized for Padma tomatoes,
especially for detecting multiple ripening stages and defects under
real-time, local conditions.
Addressing these gaps is essential for enhanced productivity, reduced
waste, and improved profitability for Sri Lankan farmers.

===== SLIDE 8 =====
To design and develop an AI-based real-time system to detect, classify, and sort Padma
tomatoes based on ripeness stage and visible defects, and to estimate remaining shelf life.
FINAL GOAL
8

===== SLIDE 9 =====
To collect and label a local dataset of more than 9000 Padma tomato
images covering four ripeness stages and one defect class under real farm
and post-harvest lighting conditions.
To develop and train a Deep Learning model that performs multi-class
classification and defect detection.
To design and construct a mechanical conveyor prototype mechanism that
automatically sorts tomatoes according to detected ripeness stage and
defects.
To integrate and evaluate a shelf-life prediction and KPI monitoring system
that estimates remaining freshness (in days) and displays class distribution,
defect ratio, and throughput on a live dashboard.

These graphs show the model learned
well on the new dataset. The training and
validation losses drop quickly in the first
epochs and then level off (better box
placement and class predictions).
Validation loss stays low and does not
rise again, so there is no sign of

===== SLIDE 21 =====
This Precision–Recall graph shows the model
now works well for all five classes. Green and
red are still the strongest (about 0.99 AP).
Defect detection has improved the most, from
about 0.19 to about 0.93 AP, so defects are no
longer a weak point. Overall mAP@0.5 is
about 0.96, and the F1 curve peaks at about
0.91 at a confidence of about 0.53.

K E Y  T A K E A W A Y S
• Larger, more balanced dataset: about 2,000 more annotated tomatoes, and every class now has more than 2,100 instances.
• Higher overall accuracy: mAP@0.5 rose from 0.90 to 0.96.
• Defect detection greatly improved: defect AP went from 0.19 to 0.93, so it is no longer the weakest class.
• More stable and confident: the F1 score is higher and the best confidence threshold rose from 0.39 to 0.53.
CONCLUSION The new YOLOv8 model is more accurate and reliable across all five ripeness and defect classes. The
biggest gain is in defect detection.
