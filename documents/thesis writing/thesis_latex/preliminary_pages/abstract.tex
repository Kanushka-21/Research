\section*{Abstract}

Many tomatoes are lost after harvest in Sri Lanka because the fruit is graded by hand. Hand grading is slow, differs from one worker to another, and bruises the fruit. This research built and tested a real-time system that uses a camera and a moving belt to grade and sort tomatoes of the local Padma variety (\textit{Solanum lycopersicum}) into four ripening stages and a defect group.

A dataset of 7{,}849 images was collected at a commercial collecting centre in Bandarawela. Each fruit was labelled as one of five classes: green, breaker, turning, red or defect. The images were split 70:20:10 into training, validation and held-out test sets. A YOLOv8-medium detector was trained on this dataset and tested once on the held-out set. It reached a precision of 0.906, a recall of 0.922 and a mAP@50 of 0.936, and it processed each frame in 6.7~ms. To give one class per fruit, the system tracks each fruit across frames and takes a confidence-weighted vote.

A low-cost conveyor was built with a stepper-driven belt, an overhead camera, four infrared sensors and four servo gates, all controlled by an ESP32 board. Each gate opens only when the fruit reaches that gate's own sensor, so sorting does not depend on a guessed belt speed. In live trials all five classes were classified correctly, the commands reached the hardware, and the gates sorted the four ripening classes while defect fruit passed to a reject stream.

A 31-day study of 100 fruit gave a remaining shelf life for each class: green 31, breaker 29, turning 24, red 12 and defect 0 days. These values agreed closely with an earlier published study on the same variety. Every result is saved to a database and shown on a live dashboard with throughput, class share, defect ratio and mean shelf life. The work shows that low-cost hardware and a single camera can grade this local variety and give a packhouse both a physical sort and batch-level information. \\

\noindent \textit{\textbf{Keywords:} Padma tomato; YOLOv8; post-harvest sorting; conveyor automation; shelf-life estimation}
